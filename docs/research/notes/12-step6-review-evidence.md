## The warnings thread

The row in [Daemon and CLI](../../decisions.md#daemon-and-cli) that starts "The daemon writes its warnings to stderr from one thread":

With stderr a pipe that was full, a daemon whose `ranking.json` did not parse had not bound its socket after 10 s, because the start waited to write the warning. With the thread, the same daemon bound after 1.02 s and answered `status` (reproduced on 2026-10-17). While Quit's wait for its replies ended with the second its warnings took, a Quit with stderr full lost its reply in 9 of 10 runs with the daemon on one CPU, and with a second for each wait it lost none of 20. While the last line was a write of its own after the wait, a second daemon whose stderr was full was still running 10 s later, and through the thread it exited 1 after 1.02 s (each reproduced on 2026-10-17). While the line of a daemon whose warnings thread did not start was a write that waited for stderr, a daemon that could start no thread, under an `RLIMIT_NPROC` of 0, with stderr full was still running 10 s later, waiting in `pipe_write`, and with the line written only when stderr has room it exited 1 after 0.005 s (each measured on 2026-10-17). That check came before the write, so a daemon stopped between the two while another writer filled stderr waited in `pipe_write` until stderr drained (round 5 review, reproduced on 2026-10-17). Stopped at its write while another writer filled stderr, a daemon whose write the timer cuts off exited 1 after 1.001 s in 3 of 3 runs, where one that wrote only when stderr had room was still in `pipe_write` after 20 s (each measured on 2026-10-17). Started with `SIGALRM` blocked, a mask that a process inherits across exec, a daemon whose write the timer cuts off, under an `RLIMIT_NPROC` of 0 with stderr full, was still in `pipe_write` after 6 s with the signal pending (round 6 review, reproduced on 2026-10-18), and once the write unblocked the signal it exited 1 after 1.02 to 1.07 s in 3 of 3 runs (measured on 2026-10-18).
